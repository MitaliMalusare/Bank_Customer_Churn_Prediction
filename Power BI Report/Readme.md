## **Objective**
The goal of this analysis is to understand the factors that lead to customer churn in the bank. By analyzing the features associated with customers who left, we aim to identify trends and develop strategies to retain customers.

![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/c428f68f-a5ec-4dd1-9b5c-582f7c3a5fa7)

## **Churn Distribution**
The churn rate for the bank stands at **15%**, meaning approximately 15% of the customers have left the bank. This highlights an opportunity to focus on improving customer retention strategies.

### **Key Takeaways:**
- **Churn Rate**: The churn rate of the bank is around **15%**.
- **Low Activity Customers**: Customers with low activity (e.g., minimal transactions or interactions) are **63%** more likely to churn.
- **Age and Balance**: Customers older than **40 years** with a balance of **less than 20k** are more likely to churn.

# **Customer Segmentation & Churn Analysis**

## **Customer Segmentation**

### **Age Group 31-40**
Customers in the **31-40 age group**, with **lower credit scores** and **lower balances**, tend to have a higher likelihood of churning. These customers may require more targeted retention strategies, such as personalized offers or better financial products to improve engagement.

![Age Group 31-40 Churn Analysis](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/26fca9d3-6414-4dd2-a4f9-7c45d614d0a5)

### **Age Group 51-60**
Customers in the **51-60 age group** have the **highest churn rate (56%)**. These customers typically have **lower credit scores** but **higher balances**, suggesting that while they may be financially stable, their engagement with the bank is low. This group may benefit from loyalty programs, targeted financial advice, or other strategies to keep them engaged.

![Age Group 51-60 Churn Analysis](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/e5d82fda-fb0f-4314-b2d0-7a718f50dc0c)

## **Key Features Influencing Churn**

Several features show significant differences between customers who have churned and those who have stayed with the bank:

### 1. **Account Balance**
Churned customers tend to have **lower account balances**. Customers with minimal deposits or account activity may be more likely to leave the bank.

### 2. **Tenure**
The **length of time** a customer has been with the bank influences their likelihood of leaving. New customers or those with shorter tenures may have higher churn rates.

### 3. **Credit Score**
Customers with **lower credit scores** tend to churn more, as they may feel less engaged with the bank's offerings or feel excluded from premium financial products.

### 4. **Age**
**Older customers** (particularly those aged **51-60**) have a higher churn rate, despite having higher account balances. This suggests that **engagement** might be the key factor for retention in this demographic.

## **Business Insights and Recommendations**

### 1. **Targeted Retention Campaigns**
Based on the churn analysis, it is crucial to design **targeted marketing campaigns** for customers in the **31-40** and **51-60** age groups, especially those with low activity and low balances.

### 2. **Loyalty Programs**
Offering **loyalty programs** or personalized services to customers with higher balances could help reduce churn, especially for the **51-60** age group.

### 3. **Customer Engagement**
Improving **customer engagement** for customers with low activity, especially those in the **31-40** age range, can significantly reduce churn.
